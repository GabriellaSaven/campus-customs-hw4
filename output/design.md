# Design Log (Problem 10)

Short, concrete log of styling changes and why each should help customers stay and buy.

## Palette — "New Haven", not just blue & white
- Yale blue anchor, **warm cream** page background and **off-white** surfaces (not stark
  white), with small accents of **brick red, stone grey, muted green** (New Haven
  brick, stone, campus greens), and **brass/gold** lines.
- *Why:* warmer, more premium and local than generic blue/white — feels like a real
  shop, not a template, so people linger.

## Typography — academic / editorial
- **Playfair Display** (high-contrast editorial serif) for big headings — the campus-
  newspaper masthead look — with Crimson Pro for smaller headings and clean Inter for
  body/product details; thin "stationery" rules.
- *Why:* reads like a campus magazine / old bookstore → trust and brand fit, not a
  trendy startup.

## Homepage — a New Haven storefront, geographically grounded
- New Haven / campus language ("For library days, game days, and everything in
  between"; "From Science Hill to Wooster Square"; "Built for Beinecke mornings and
  Toad's nights") and a **"New Haven picks"** section ("For Old Campus", "For game day",
  "For your first New Haven winter", "For parents visiting").
- *Why:* speaks to the actual buyer's life → emotional relevance → conversion.

## Architectural cues — collegiate gothic (whole-site vibe)
- **Warm Yale-sandstone ashlar wall** across the page (cut stone blocks in running
  bond, matching Yale's actual stone color) — not a near-black wall (too dark) nor a
  faint map (didn't read).
- A **glowing stained-glass Gothic window** in the hero (lancets, rose window, stone
  tracery) with a **candlelight flicker** animation; a **tower skyline** (Harkness-like)
  along the hero base; a dark stone **hero** and **footer foundation** (gold cornice +
  tower) for drama against the warm wall.
- **Carved-stone header plaques** — section titles sit on engraved slate plaques with a
  gold edge.
- **Climbing ivy** that creeps the full height of both page edges (seamless vine).
- Product cards are **warm parchment panels** that lift off the sandstone; photos stay
  clean and whole. The CTA band is a **leaded-glass stone panel** (gold + navy), not a
  flat blue box. Falling leaves + candlelight-gold buttons/accents throughout.
- *Removed as "too literal/off": clipping product photos into arches, the pointed-arch
  arcade divider.*
- *Why:* unmistakably Yale/New Haven stone architecture, dramatic but readable, with
  product photos legible as spotlit items.

## Editorial product cards
- Bigger imagery, more breathing room, name + short human description, occasional
  **"staff pick" / "our favorite" / "best for fall"** stamps.
- *Why:* less grid-y, more curated → each item feels worth considering → more clicks.

## Microcopy & chatbot personality
- Search: "What are you looking for?"; buy button: "Secure the bag"; Handsome Dan opens
  with "Woof woof" and talks like a friendly student shop assistant, not an AI.
- *Why:* human, on-brand voice lowers friction and is more persuasive.

## Seasonal feel (changes with the date)
- **Animated** weather overlay that matches the date: drifting leaves in fall, snow in
  winter, petals in spring, sun/seagulls in summer, caps at commencement, footballs for
  The Game — plus a seasonal ribbon line. (Respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.)
- *Why:* the site feels alive and timely → reasons to come back.

## New Haven map background (identifiable)
- Replaced the elm-tree footer with a faint, **recognizable downtown New Haven map** —
  the historic **Nine Square Plan** (3×3 grid with **The Green** at center, labeled
  Grove/Elm/Chapel/George and York/College/Temple/Church) — as the whole-page backdrop.
- *Why:* unmistakably New Haven, grounding the shop in place without shouting.

## Search, cart & featured
- A **search bar in the top nav** ("What are you looking for?"); a **cart icon with a
  live count badge**; product pages "Add to bag" and the cart checks out with **"Secure
  the bag"** (recording real orders). Home shows exactly **3** "Handsome Dan's current
  picks."
- *Why:* standard retail affordances reduce friction and move people toward purchase.

## Subtle motion
- Product image lifts on hover; underlines animate in; the **cart count pops** when it
  changes.
- *Why:* small polish that makes browsing feel responsive and high-quality.

## Footer — local
- A faint New Haven street map plus "Made with too much coffee in New Haven, CT." /
  "Yale gear for people who actually went to school here."
- *Why:* reinforces authenticity and locality at the point people decide to trust you.
